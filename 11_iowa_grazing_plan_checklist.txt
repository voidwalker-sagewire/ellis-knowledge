SOURCE: Grazing Plan Checklist/Worksheet (Iowa)
PUBLISHER: USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service, December 2018

---

PURPOSE

A structured intake worksheet an NRCS planner or producer uses to capture everything
needed to design or evaluate a grazing plan in one pass - objectives, soils, forage,
weeds, herd health, fencing, and watering math all in one document.

PRODUCER OBJECTIVES (checklist categories - useful as a self-assessment prompt)

- Increase forage diversity / Increase forage yields
- Improve grazing utilization / Use less hay or silage
- Increase profit / Improve water quality
- Increase number of livestock / Improve animal performance
- Grass-based dairy / Convert CRP (Conservation Reserve Program land)
- Reduce reliance on outside sources (rented pasture, feed supplement)
- Extend the grazing season (move toward year-long grazing)
- Grass-finished, organic, or direct-market niche markets
Also asks: is this operation more of a business or a hobby (spectrum scale) - framing
matters for how aggressively to invest in infrastructure/management.

SOILS BASICS TO TRACK
- Last year soils were tested
- Last year lime was applied
- Last year fertilizer was applied

FORAGE INFORMATION (best captured on a pasture map)
- Current grazing method: continuous, rotational, MIG (management-intensive grazing),
  or high-density
- Forage species present in each paddock
- Estimated % tree cover per paddock (if applicable - silvopasture-type systems)
- Hay fields, labeled with forage species mix (e.g. "Alf/Brome" = alfalfa/bromegrass)
- Whether hay fields are ever grazed, and approximately when/how long
- Cropland acres gleaned (grazed post-harvest)
- Grazing season extension methods in use (stockpiling forage, planting turnips,
  planting forage sorghum, etc.)
- Additional tracts available for grazing/haying beyond the core system

WEED/BRUSH PROBLEMS
List by paddock. Common Iowa examples cited: Canada thistle, multiflora rose, black
locust.

HERD/PADDOCK HEALTH FLAGS
Poor rates of gain, breeding problems, calving problems - these often trace back to
grazing management gaps and are worth cross-referencing against forage inventory and
pasture condition scores.

PLAN MAP ITEMS (checklist for what to document on the pasture map)
- Pasture Condition Score (attach score sheet - see NRCS PCS guide)
- Existing and planned fence lines
- Whether fencing is electrified, and whether temporary fence is used
- Perimeter fence type (barbed, hi-tensile, woven) and wire count (1-8+)
- Cross-fence type and wire count
- Erosion or other concerns needing follow-up/discussion
- Fields the producer wants to convert to pasture or hay
- Stream crossings, heavy-use protection areas, riparian areas
- Adjacent CRP acres that could be grazed (typically 1/2 annually or all every other
  year under CRP grazing rules)

LIVESTOCK DEFAULT WEIGHTS (reference table, useful for AU calculations)
Cattle cow: 1,300 lbs | Bison calf (not weaned): 400 lbs | Elk (75% AUE - Animal Unit
Equivalent): yearling/replacement 1,050 lbs, bulls 2,100 lbs | Stockers/backgrounders:
750 lbs | Sheep ewe: 140 lbs | Replacement ewe lambs: 100 lbs | Ram: 160 lbs | Market
lambs: 90 lbs | Horses (mares/studs/geldings): 1,100 lbs | Horse yearlings: 800 lbs |
Spanish goat nannies: 135 lbs | Boer goat kids: 70 lbs | Dairy goat billies: 200 lbs |
Alpacas/llamas: 150-300 lbs.

PRODUCTION CALENDAR FIELDS TO DEFINE
- Grazing months for the system (yearlong or specific months)
- Breeding season type (year-round, 90-day, 60-day, AI, embryo transfer, other)
- Birthing months
- Weaning months
- Number of herds managed separately (1, 2, 3, 4+)

WATERING MATH - TWO METHODS

Individual Watering Math (for intensive grazing systems where individuals water alone):
Pipeline Capacity (gpm) Q = [(A x W) - C] / 720
Where A = number of animals, W = daily water need per pair (default 20 gal/day/pair
for a cow/calf pair, adjustable for other stock), C = tank capacity in gallons.

Herd Watering Math (assumes 3 watering events/day, 30 min per event) - use the GREATER
of these two results:
Q = [(A x W / 3) - C] / 30   (checks: is the trough large enough?)
Q = A x W / 240              (checks: is the supply rate itself sufficient?)

WATER SOURCE CHECKLIST
- Source type: pond, well, creek, rural water, or other
- Are sources dependable? Are troughs frost-free?
- Pump type if applicable: electric, solar, ram, nose (animal-activated), other
- Pressure tank location/elevation and depth to static water level in well
- Pressure tank on/off PSI settings (current vs. possible adjustment)
- Pressure tank capacity in gallons
- Pond water elevations: trough outlet elevation vs. highest water elevation (matters
  for gravity-feed feasibility)

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY: This worksheet is essentially the "everything you need to know
before designing a real grazing plan" checklist - useful as a self-audit even without
formal NRCS program participation, since it forces documentation of forage, water,
fencing, and livestock health data in one place before making fencing/water
infrastructure investment decisions.
