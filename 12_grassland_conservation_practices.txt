PURPOSE

A menu of standard NRCS conservation practices a landowner/operator can consider for
grassland/grazing management, each with a description, maintenance requirements, and
a yes/no self-assessment prompt. Useful as a checklist against Ellis's other grazing
knowledge - if a producer is missing one of these, it's a candidate improvement.

ACCESS CONTROL
Limiting the time or season that vehicles/livestock can access water bodies,
environmentally sensitive areas, or hazardous areas.
Maintenance: periodic barrier inspection and repair.

BRUSH MANAGEMENT
Reducing/eliminating undesirable vegetation to increase vigor, amount, and quality of
desired vegetation, and increase wildlife habitat.
Maintenance: spot-treat individual plants/areas needing re-treatment as needed.

FENCE
A constructed barrier controlling animal traffic patterns to reduce erosion and control
access, permitting vegetation recovery or stockpiling.
Maintenance: routine inspection as an ongoing program; inspect specifically in spring
after snowmelt and after storm events for weaknesses, breaks, or malfunctions.

FORAGE HARVEST MANAGEMENT
Timely cutting/removal of forage for optimized yield, quality, stand life, pest control,
and wildlife habitat maintenance.
Maintenance: clear fields of debris before harvest (protects machinery); don't cut
while dew/rain is still on leaves; mow most recent seedings ahead of older stands.

GRADE STABILIZATION STRUCTURE
A structure controlling channel grade in natural/constructed water courses - can double
as a livestock water source, wildlife habitat, and gully erosion control.
Maintenance: requires a documented operation/maintenance plan with periodic inspection
and prompt repair/replacement of damaged components.

HEAVY USE PROTECTION AREA
Stabilizing areas frequented by vehicles or livestock to reduce erosion near critical
water bodies, improve water quality, and improve herd health.
Maintenance: documented operation/maintenance plan reviewed with the landowner/operator.

NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT
Proper placement of the correct nutrient amount at the correct plant growth stage -
increases forage production, reduces nutrient loss to surface/groundwater, increases
production and profit.
Maintenance: equipment must be calibrated for uniform distribution at planned rates;
document actual application rates; changes in animal numbers or feed management
require re-analysis of nutrient needs.

PASTURE AND HAY PLANTING
Establishing desired native/introduced forages to supply feed during normally
low-production periods, reduce erosion/runoff, improve water quality, increase carbon
sequestration.
Maintenance: inspect/calibrate planting equipment for proper rate/distribution/depth;
monitor growth for water stress; cutting, herbicides, or grazing management may be
needed to control undesirable plants during establishment.

PEST MANAGEMENT
Reducing impacts of invasive species, weeds, and pest invasions while minimizing
impacts to soil/water resources and non-target plants/animals.
Maintenance: operator responsible for proper implementation and equipment maintenance;
plans should be reviewed/updated periodically; a safety plan should be developed.

PRESCRIBED BURNING
Used to increase quantity, quality, and vigor of certain desired plant species, and
reduce competition from undesired species.
Maintenance: burn strictly according to a prescribed burn plan; monitor smoke,
liability, and safety/health precautions.

PRESCRIBED GRAZING
Managing vegetation harvest with grazing animals to maintain/improve the desired plant
community and groundwater quality, reduce erosion, and improve wildlife cover.
Maintenance: regularly monitor data and grazing records to confirm objectives are
being met, adjusting the grazing plan as needed.

RIPARIAN FOREST BUFFER
Trees/shrubs established adjacent to a stream, lake, or water body to improve water
quality, reduce sediment delivery, create aquatic shade, and mitigate flood damage.
Maintenance: periodic tree maintenance and harvest; as the buffer matures, tree
harvesting itself becomes important for plant health and buffer function (i.e. this
isn't "plant and forget").

WATER WELL
A well constructed to an aquifer providing water for domestic livestock and general
farm water needs.
Maintenance: keep well log and permit documents on file; any maintenance/modification/
repair should be done by a Certified Well Driller or Certified Pump Installer.

WATERING FACILITY
Permanent or portable tanks/troughs increasing even distribution of grazing animals,
promoting even grazing, and providing a water source for wildlife too.
Maintenance: documented operation/maintenance plan; keep tank/trough clear of debris,
algae, sludge; check for leaks; check for erosion in adjacent areas (a sign of an
undersized or poorly-placed facility).

WILDLIFE HABITAT MANAGEMENT (Uplands or Wetlands)
Rehabilitating degraded uplands/wetlands, or creating/enhancing areas for wildlife food
and cover.
Maintenance: requires a plan addressing wildlife needs, food source establishment,
vegetation management, acceptable uses, and timing/operation of any water control
structures.

WINDBREAK/SHELTERBELT ESTABLISHMENT
Linear plantings of multiple tree/shrub rows providing shelter for structures,
wildlife, livestock, and people; improves air quality; provides noise/visual screening;
manages snow deposition.
Maintenance: control competing vegetation; protect planting from livestock/wildlife;
replace dead trees; supplemental water may be needed for establishment; protect from
fire with firebreaks; inspect at least every six months.

PASTURE CONDITION SCORE (PCS) - tie-in to the fuller NRCS PCS guide
Framed here as the master diagnostic: "Could your PCS be improved with conservation
practices?" This sheet is essentially a menu to consult AFTER running a Pasture
Condition Score assessment - each low-scoring PCS indicator points toward specific
practices from this list as the corrective action.
