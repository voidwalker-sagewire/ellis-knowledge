SOURCE: E528G - Improved Grazing Management on Pasture for Plant Productivity and
Health with Monitoring Activities (Conservation Stewardship Program Enhancement)
+ Washington State Supplement to E528 references
PUBLISHER: USDA, Conservation Stewardship Program, April 2022 (WA supplement Nov 2022)

---

WHAT THIS IS

A specific USDA Conservation Stewardship Program (CSP) "enhancement" activity built on
top of NRCS Conservation Practice Standard 528 (Prescribed Grazing). It's a formal
program requiring documentation and a qualifying professional's involvement - useful to
know as a reference framework even outside formal program enrollment, since it lays out
a rigorous version of what a complete grazing management plan looks like.

CRITERIA (what "improved grazing management" means under this standard)

- A written plan matching forage quantity/quality produced to grazing/browsing demand
- Herbage removal in accordance with site production limits, plant growth rate, forage
  plant physiological needs, and animal nutritional needs
- Adjusting intensity, frequency, timing, and duration of grazing/browsing (with
  sufficient recovery time) to meet written objectives for plant communities and
  associated resources
- Planned deferment (non-grazing period under 1 year) and/or rest (1+ year) for
  critical plant-need periods: post-planting/renovation, severe drought, etc.
- Managing grazing/browsing to maintain adequate cover on sensitive areas: riparian
  areas, wetlands, habitats of concern, karst areas
- Managing livestock movement based on plant growth rate, available forage, and
  allowable utilization target, with contingency plans for episodic disturbance events
- A soils test for organic matter and nutrient analysis (via a land grant university or
  accredited lab) on applicable acres
- Fertilizer/soil amendments applied according to current soil test results when plant
  vigor needs improvement
- Guidance followed from a Certified Forage and Grassland Professional, Certified
  Range Management Consultant, Certified Professional in Range Management, an
  approved NRCS Technical Service Provider, or a non-affiliated consultant with a
  relevant bachelor's+ degree and 5+ years of pastureland conservation planning
  experience

REQUIRED GRAZING MANAGEMENT PLAN COMPONENTS (useful as a template regardless of
program enrollment)

1. Producer goals, objectives, and resource concerns
2. Location and condition of structural improvements
3. Watering sites with availability, quantity, and quality
4. Forage inventory
5. Forage-animal balance sheet
6. Grazing plan for livestock movement
7. Contingency plan
8. Monitoring plan

MONITORING REQUIREMENTS UNDER THIS STANDARD

A qualified professional is expected to:
1. Select a monitoring site in each forage type/mixture on the enrolled acreage,
   assessed with the Pasture Condition Scoring (PCS) tool
2. Conduct and document PCS assessments at those sites
3. Develop a written recommendation covering grazing duration/intensity, based on
   desired health/productivity objectives, addressing cover, litter, and canopy needed
   to maintain/improve infiltration and soil health while reducing compaction and other
   identified resource concerns

Ongoing participant responsibilities: identify key grazing areas and key forage
species; monitor pastures for grazing utilization; keep pasture/herd in/out records;
complete a forage utilization job sheet at season's end; document adjustments needed
to maintain feed/forage balance.

End-of-program documentation package: PCS score sheets with field notes/locations,
soil test analysis, written professional recommendations and follow-up actions,
pasture/herd in/out dates, completed forage utilization job sheet, animal/forage
balance sheet, and written modifications to the grazing/monitoring plan addressing
identified resource concerns.

WASHINGTON STATE SUPPLEMENTAL REFERENCES (illustrates the kind of state-level technical
resources that typically exist to support this work - a producer in any state should
look for their own state's equivalent NRCS Field Office Technical Guide (FOTG) folder)

- National Range and Pasture Handbook (Title 190, Part 645) - the umbrella federal
  reference document
- Ecological Site Descriptions - found in each state's NRCS Field Office Technical
  Guide (FOTG), Section 2
- Web Soil Survey - for identifying ecological sites tied to specific planning-unit
  soils (websoilsurvey.sc.egov.usda.gov)
- Interpreting Indicators of Rangeland Health (Technical Reference 1734-6) - a
  rangeland-focused health assessment framework, complementary to Pasture Condition
  Scoring
- Range/Pasture Technical Notes covering native/non-native grass species critical
  growth periods, fencing design worksheets, and riparian grazing management
  (e.g. "Herbaceous Stubble Height as a Warning of Impending Cattle Grazing Damage to
  Riparian Areas" - Hall & Bryant 1995; "Managing Grazing of Riparian Areas in the
  Intermountain Region" - Clary & Webster 1989)
- State wildlife agency Priority Habitats and Species (PHS) databases, useful for
  identifying wildlife/habitat concerns relevant to a grazing plan

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Even outside formal CSP enrollment, this standard is a good "gold standard checklist"
for a genuinely complete grazing management plan - most producers doing serious
regenerative/rotational grazing are already doing much of this informally (forage
inventory, watering plan, PCS-style monitoring), and formalizing it into a written plan
with the 8 core components listed above creates both a better management tool and (if
ever desired) a program-ready document.
